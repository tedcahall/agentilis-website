An Executive Job Search, From Screening to Submission, Run by an AI Agent

A senior technology executive needed a job search that ran every day without becoming a second job. Done by hand, that meant scanning job boards, rewriting a resume for every posting, filling in the same forms again and again, and tracking it all in an inbox. Now an agent does the back-office work. It screens thousands of postings, tailors a resume and cover letter for each role, files applications within rules the executive wrote, tracks the pipeline and keeps interview stories ready. The executive decides what goes out.

What the Agent Does
  1. Builds a factual career record by interviewing the executive in depth, role by role and credential by credential
  2. Scans job boards, staffing firm sites and company career pages every hour, and logs each posting in a screening ledger
  3. Screens each role against the executive's written preferences, such as pay floor, location and excluded employers, and rates the fit high, medium or low
  4. Saves the posting and maps each requirement to a fact in the career record, noting every gap
  5. Tailors a resume and cover letter for that role, in Word and PDF, in a clean format that applicant tracking systems can read
  6. Fills in the application from the approved answer sheet and the career record, and submits only within the rules below
  7. Saves the confirmation page and updates the tracker with the status and next step
  8. Reports every submission, and every medium fit, to the executive the same day
  9. Keeps a set of interview stories in STAR form (situation, task, action, result), ready for screening calls and interviews
The Hard Cases
  • A requirement the record can't support: the agent never invents, inflates or rounds up. If a core requirement has no evidence, the role is skipped or reported. Gaps go to the executive, never into the cover letter.
  • A question the record doesn't answer: a non-compete clause, a travel commitment or an unexpected attestation stops the form. The executive sees the question and decides.
  • The same job on several boards: the screening ledger is checked before anything is evaluated, so a posting seen twice is never applied to twice.
  • Proof of every submission: after every submission the agent saves the confirmation page, and an application counts as submitted only when that confirmation is on file.
Guardrail

The executive, not the agent, decides what goes out. The executive wrote a standing rule: the agent may submit on its own only when a role meets the written preferences, every line of the resume and cover letter traces to the career record, every form answer comes from the approved answer sheet or the record, and the fit is rated high. A medium fit is reported for the executive to decide. A low fit is skipped. Anything outside that rule needs the executive's explicit yes in their own chat, naming that job, after they have seen the full packet: the resume, the cover letter and every form answer. No other agent can give that yes. Skills tests, attestations, payments, CAPTCHAs and verification codes always go to the executive. Messages to recruiters, replies to employers and withdrawals are drafted, then sent only after a yes. Only the executive can change or revoke the rule.

What It Means for You

A job search is mostly back-office work: searching, screening, tailoring, filling in forms and tracking. An agent can carry all of it every hour, from a record you have verified, and bring you only the decisions that need you.
