AI Agents Mapped Where AI Fits Across 12 Professional-Services Industries, Then Ranked Them

Before an AI consulting team chose where to start, it needed to know what small firms would actually buy, in which industries, and in what order. Done by hand, that means reading across a dozen industries and comparing ideas that all sound promising. In our own operations, AI agents wrote up and scored 108 AI use cases across 12 professional-services industries, from real estate brokers and architecture firms to dental and veterinary practices. Every use case was scored for adoption fit and competition, every industry got a priority score, and the result became a full plan with a playbook for each industry. The plan went from first draft to its 23rd version in 8 days, and people made the calls on where to start.

What the Agents Do
  1. Research how small professional-services firms buy: who decides, how fast, and what makes the return clear enough to say yes
  2. Write up every use case the same way: the problem, the solution, the value, the likely pushback, the competitors and a suggested price
  3. Score adoption fit, the share of firms likely to buy within 12 to 18 months, on four factors: how widely the pain is felt, how clear the return is, how much friction stands in the way, and whether the market already buys it elsewhere
  4. Rate competition Low, Medium or High under written rules for how crowded each market is
  5. Cross the two in a 3 by 3 matrix that rates each use case go early, position carefully, or pass
  6. Rank the 12 industries by priority score, name the top 3 as entry targets and the 4th as the next to enter
  7. Write a playbook for each industry: the first use case to lead with, the ideal client, the go-to-market approach, pricing and competitive notes
  8. Set prices: a recommended monthly price for each use case, and three bundles that grow from a first win to a full practice
  9. Build the deliverables: the master plan, deeper plans for two industries, a channel strategy, slide decks and a revenue model
The Hard Cases
  • Close scores: optometry practices scored 107.2, one point behind insurance agencies, and missed the top 3. The plan names optometry as the next industry to enter once the first engagements are proven.
  • The most valuable is not always first: law firms are the highest-value industry by billing rate but ranked fifth, largely because only 5 of their 6 use cases rated go early, not all 6.
Guardrail

Every score follows a written rule that is printed in the plan, so anyone can check how a use case or an industry earned its place. Adoption fit is labeled as an estimate of likely buyers, not a market forecast. The ranking is a recommendation. People reviewed the drafts and decided which industries to pursue, and the plan sets conditions before entering any industry, including association contacts, a warm introduction to a practice owner, and a first offer that is scoped and priced.
