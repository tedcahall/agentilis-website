Case Studies

Founding-Era Documents, Read From the Page Image and Printed Exactly as Written, for Eduba Press

Eduba Press publishes Faith and the Founding, a four-volume reference set in its Primary Source Series. Many of its documents survive only as page images of books printed in the 1700s, and the imperfections of that old printing mean OCR cannot transcribe them correctly. Agents now read the page image itself, keep the original spelling and punctuation, compare every paragraph of the book with its source, and change the book until the two agree. The editor rules on anything that needs judgment.

What the Agents Do
  1. Ties each document to one cited source at an established institution, and confirms the link serves that document
  2. Finds where the document starts and ends on the scan, and measures the offset between scan page and printed page for each book
  3. Crops each page to its type and reads it as two overlapping halves, then compares the overlap as a second reading and settles every disagreement against the image
  4. Transcribes to fixed rules: long s set as s, the printer's space before ; : ? ! kept, spelling and misprints kept, line-end hyphens rejoined, running heads and page numbers left out, footnotes moved to the end
  5. Reports the last six words of every page and checks them against the next page's opening, so nothing drops at a page break
  6. Assembles the pages into paragraphs and confirms every join across a page break is present
  7. Compares every paragraph of the book with its source, and lists each difference the editor has not approved
  8. Closes a difference by changing the book to match the source, word by word. Anything that needs judgment is skipped and reported
  9. Numbers every paragraph and builds an index keyed to document and paragraph
The Hard Cases
  • Wrong words that read well: on the 1776 sermon, the two half-page readings disagreed on “use” and “die”, and on “stiled” and “called”. Each word fits the sentence, so no text check could catch it. The second reading did, and the page image settled it.
  • Printer's errors that stay: one printing of Jefferson sets “religlons”, “covernment” and “infalliable”. They stay as printed, because the set promises readers the document as it was, not a corrected version of it.
  • Where a page really ends: the machine text does not always show where a page stops, so every page reports its last six words from the page image, and they are checked against the opening of the next page.
  • Sensitive history: some pages cover religious persecution, which general AI safety filters can flag. The agents state the scholarly purpose and work in smaller sections, so these pages are transcribed by the same model as every other page, never handed to a fallback.
Guardrail

The source always wins, and the editor decides. Agents make no change to the text outside categories the editor has approved, and each approval is recorded with the date and time it was given. Every word change needs the page image. A row that needs judgment is skipped and reported, never guessed. Each document is checked only against the source cited in the back of the book. Bringing in a second source needs the editor's permission, every time.
