Receipts and Invoices, From Every Inbox to the Books, for a Software Foundation

At a software foundation, receipts and invoices arrived in many places: employee inboxes, admin accounts, Gmail and Outlook. Each one had to be found, saved, named, filed, totaled and entered in the books by hand. Now an agent does it every hour, around the clock: it gathers every receipt, files it in the cloud drive, records it in a database, updates the totals, reconciles everything, and enters the invoices in QuickBooks.

What the Agent Does
  1. Checks all 7 employee and admin inboxes, Gmail and Outlook, every hour
  2. Finds receipts and invoices from approved vendors and reads the amount and account
  3. Saves the attached PDF, or creates one from the email itself when there is no attachment
  4. Files it in the cloud drive, in the right folder by vendor and year
  5. Records the charge in a SQLite database: date, vendor, amount, account, invoice number and document link
  6. Updates the spreadsheets with totals and a link to every receipt
  7. Enters the invoice in QuickBooks
  8. Reconciles the database, the spreadsheets and the filed documents, and reports any mismatch
  9. Reviews the executive mailbox daily for billing items and open actions, and reports them by text message
The Hard Cases
  • Bundled receipts: Apple puts several services on one receipt. The agent splits out each charge, keeps one shared PDF, and sends tax, unknown services and refunds for review. 185 Apple charge lines were handled this way.
  • Several accounts with one vendor: separate AWS accounts keep separate identities, so their charges never blend together.
  • Duplicates across inboxes: the same receipt often lands in more than one inbox. A charge is removed as a duplicate only when the account, invoice and line all match, never on date and amount alone.
  • Credits and refunds: negative amounts stop for review instead of being booked automatically.
Guardrail

Nothing is skipped and nothing is fixed silently. If a vendor's receipts fail to process, operations is notified immediately and that vendor stops at the failed message while the others continue, so nothing is missed. Reconciliation reports every mismatch for a person to review. A PDF created from an email body is clearly labeled as a reproduction, not the vendor's original invoice.

What It Means for You
