Chapter 1 · 2015

A weekend project becomes the official scoreboard.

Ted Cahall races cars, and he builds software. In 2015 he wanted to learn Amazon's cloud, so he picked a project he cared about: tracking points for his own races in the Mid-Atlantic Road Racing Series, the club series run by the Washington DC Region of the SCCA.

It didn't stay small. Other drivers wanted their points too. Ted wired it to the timing system's feed, then to the club's registration system to match every driver to an SCCA number. By 2016, marrspoints.com was the official points system for every class in the series. In 2018 the region gave him its Regional Executive Award for building and running it.

Chapter 2 · The catch

Every race weekend, an hour of work only one person could do.

The system worked. The process around it didn't scale. After every race weekend, 27 official result files had to be renamed to the right format, uploaded one at a time through an admin tool, published and checked. That took more than an hour, every weekend, for years.

And it all ran through one person. Underneath sat two Java applications that had been in production for more than a decade, written by the same person who was now spending his weekends feeding them files.

Chapter 3 · Early 2026

The first agent clocks in.

In January 2026, Ted brought his first AI agent online, a chief of staff named Max. A few weeks later he gave Max the race-weekend job: receive the 27 result files, log into the admin tool, rename and upload every file, publish the results and send a confirmation.

Max did it. No human touched a file. The hour of hand work was gone, and the question changed from "can an agent help with this?" to "how much of this system can agents run?"

Chapter 4 · The team forms

A team of agents takes over the code itself.

Day one
James, the Java engineer, refactored 20,000 lines of decade-old code on his first day on the job.

James went on to upgrade both Java applications and ship new features. Watts, the QA engineer, wrote more than 60 automated tests, so nothing reaches production without being checked first. Ken, the systems administrator, keeps the test environment and the AWS production servers current, and verifies every release.

Each agent has a defined job. Ted sets the direction and reviews the work, the way a manager would.

Chapter 5 · Race day

The real test: live results while the cars are still on track.

Processing official results after the race follows clear rules. Race day doesn't. Provisional results come in while racing is under way, with gaps and imperfect data, and someone has to decide what to publish, what to hold, and what's provisional rather than final.

That job now belongs to Inman, the agent named after NASCAR's most decorated crew chief. During the 2026 season, eight race weekends at Summit Point, NJMP, VIR and Mid-Ohio, Inman handles the live feed during the day and the official results after the checkered flag.

This is where agents usually fall down: not the repetitive work, but the judgment calls in a live environment with real people waiting on the answer.

What It Means for You

Every business has a marrspoints.
